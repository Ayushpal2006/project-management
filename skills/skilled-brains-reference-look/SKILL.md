# The Skilled Brains: reference look (deep frame study, 2026-10-29)

Motion graphics like **Aevy TV**, **Full Disclosure**, **Cutting Edge School**; typography like **Tharun Speaks**; B-roll from the user's Drive library (folder 1wDjUeX4GdUrgXf-meXuNofIvOOu0rrkj) first. These rules come from 8 videos sampled every 3-5 frames. Full notes were in the project files as retrain/REFERENCE_NOTES.md; copy them to `brand/references/` when available.

House rules in skilled-brains-motion-style still win: no glow, English-only on-screen text (Hinglish only where the user allows), breathing space, less text, never touch the course/batch part, one palette per video taken from the footage. Take layout, motion and timing from the references, never their colours.

## 0. Pacing targets (measured)
- New picture every ~3 s on average. B-roll/graphic shots 1-2.5 s (median ~2 s). A-roll stretches 2-5 s. Speaker on screen only 25-50% of runtime.
- Sub-second cuts only in montage bursts (5-9 cuts in 5 s), then settle.
- A-roll has two framings (wide and ~1.3x tight); cut between them on sentence boundaries. Punch-ins are cuts, not animated zooms.

## 1. Typography (Tharun Speaks)
- **Fonts:** captions and lead lines in **Nunito Black** (lowercase OK). Hero words in **Kurdis** ALL CAPS (its lowercase 'r' reads as 'n'), normal width, never stretched. Hand-lettered marker style only for quotes/notes, and only with a face the user approves.
- **Two layers at once:** small captions in front (lower centre, white, soft shadow, no box) + a GIANT hero word BEHIND the speaker (segment the speaker so the head occludes letters). Lockup = small lowercase LEAD line above-left + HERO caps (~60-75% frame width) + optional small TAIL line bottom-right.
- **Timing:** every word cuts on at its spoken syllable, no fade-in. Hero follows its lead by ~0.4 s; a second hero word ~0.44 s after the first. Hold the lockup ~4 s (longer than the words); it leaves on the next cut, no exit animation.
- **Captions are not continuous:** drop them during gestures, pauses and filler (2-3 s gaps are fine). Vary size by importance (key word bigger/bolder than 'in', 'the').
- **Hero frequency:** about one per 40-60 s. Everything else is small captions.
- **Type can cross a cut:** build a headline over B-roll word by word, then cut to A-roll and keep it in place behind the speaker ~5 s.
- **Hook quote:** build word by word, ACCUMULATING into a 2-line block; then a hand-drawn red line strikes/underlines the key words (0.3 s draw). No glow pulse.
- **Split hero:** two words flanking the head (LEFT word, RIGHT word) at eye level.
- **Key-phrase stack over B-roll:** 2 words on 2 lines, huge, tight leading, over desaturated B-roll; or hero in the accent colour, rough condensed, slight tilt, with the subject standing in front of the text.
- **Section heads:** small numbered line ('1. What is this skill actually about?') typing word by word under a line-art graphic.
- **Glass bubble on hands:** a frosted-glass circle with a short lowercase line, tracked to the speaker's hand when he 'holds' an idea.
- Spell-check every word.

## 2. Explainer motion graphics (Aevy TV / Full Disclosure)
- **Grounds:** cream paper (~#EEEAE0) or light grey with grain, or near-black/charcoal; faint grid lines. Flat fills, thin 1-2 px lines, tiny labels, lots of empty space. ONE accent colour for the thing that matters; everything else muted.
- **Build order (always):** ground -> small title fades in (0.4 s) -> main shape draws on (0.5-0.7 s) -> data grows one item at a time on the VO -> label types on letter by letter -> hold 1.5-2.5 s -> hard cut out.
- **Camera never stops:** slow linear drift/push during holds; eased 0.5-1 s truck/tilt/pull-out between beats. Prefer one continuous move through a laid-out world (gallery of cards along a scale, timeline truck) over slide-by-slide pops.
- **Show 1, then many:** one object, then copies drop in while the camera pulls out until the frame is a pattern.
- **Abstract -> real:** figure-ground map or isometric model, colour it to carry the point, then match-cut to real footage of the same place.
- **Maps:** flat India/state or figure-ground street maps on a faint grid, hatched vs solid fills, small-caps labels, fills animated one region at a time; pins with a thin frame + pointer, pull out to reveal many.
- **Isometric data:** grey extruded blocks with flat 3-tone shading on a coloured plot; one flash of darker accent on the key word; coloured outline strokes trace details like a technical drawing; big number along the shape's edge.
- **Bars and scales:** bars grow fast from the axis (0.3-0.4 s ease-out), split bars part, the punch bar breaks the frame in the accent colour. Scales made of dots graded green->red with a sliding pointer whose value COUNTS UP as it moves; labels left behind as markers.
- **Numbers roll** (counters), never just appear.
- **Timelines:** one thin line, accent-coloured serif years, small triangle markers alternating above/below, pill tags; camera trucks ~1.5 s per marker.
- **Comparison template:** two square tiles with dotted keylines, labels typed above, bracket + wide-tracked caps label under the key tile; reuse the same template for every pair.
- **Designed objects:** custom banknote, postage-stamp price tag (perforated edge), UI selection box with corner handles, dimension lines. One evolving object beats a chart.
- **People:** real B&W cut-out (or a pile of cut-out prints popping in at slight angles) + one flat colour shape + name in a small solid tag + tiny descriptor. Never monograms unless no photo exists (flag it).
- **Evidence:** show the real article/tweet/site/paper. Fast scroll -> cursor drags a blue selection, or a black highlight sweeps word by word with the VO -> HARD CUT to a 2-4x macro of the line. Optional handwritten annotation writes itself underneath. Faint scan-line texture on screen captures. Postage-stamp card for tweets.
- **Archival/news:** inset card on black or blurred fill (keep ratio, thin border), tiny credit bottom-left, outlet logo top-left for news; B&W/greyscale = past or problem.
- **Grids:** 2x2 of dimmed footage still playing, one short white sentence per panel, thin white gutters.
- **Chapters:** dip to black (0.4 s fade, ~1.5 s black) then ONE stylised title: serif question over a duotone halftone macro texture, or a retro italic word inside a small 3D scene with objects passing in front. 4-6 s.
- **Scenario mini-film:** tell hypotheticals as 5-6 shots (wide, insert, close-up) in one consistent illustration style (halftone print, or AI painterly via Google Flow), 0.6-2 s each.
- **Documentary texture:** walking transition on matching footsteps between locations, BTS of research (papers, monitors), physical props on set with motion-tracked labels, ultra-wide handheld selfie for emotional lines.

## 3. Cards, lists, product (Cutting Edge School)
- Pastel gradient ground (lavender->mint) with faint grain, or a deep gradient for dividers and prompt/quote cards; one saturated accent (in OUR palette).
- **Name posters (hook):** portrait resolves from a gradient-map silhouette to B&W cut-out in 0.4 s; first name huge behind the head, surname smaller; 1.2 s each, straight cuts.
- **Numbered badges:** grow from a dot with slight overshoot on each spoken number, 0.35-0.7 s apart, inside thin dashed rings.
- **List card:** title resolves blur->sharp left to right (0.3 s); items blur-in one under another ~0.35 s apart; hold ~1 s. Or one item per full frame with a counter (house rule).
- **Section divider:** thin horizontal line with small 4-point sparkles sliding in; the question types on above.
- **Split layout:** speaker reframed to the right ~45%, left panel with the question as a bold heading + one bullet typing in; stays while he talks.
- **Returning tracker:** a 'Step-by-step checklist' card that comes back each section with one more item.
- **Diagrams:** flat shapes; shift focus by SCALE (focused shape grows 1.4x, others shrink); dashed arrows fan out / converge.
- **Real UI:** recreate apps/pricing pages as clean cards; one card in focus, the rest dimmed and blurred; QR tile in a corner during product mentions; sponsor segments get a thin progress bar along the bottom.
- **Photo carousel:** real proof photos, one at a time, 0.35-0.7 s each, caption under.
- Reflective episodes can stay raw (A-roll + captions only).

## 4. Tharun-style graphic worlds
- One monochrome world per video, in the video's palette: e.g. deep accent + black + white, or charcoal + white with one accent object. Monochrome AI scenes (lone figure in a light shaft), frosted-glass UI cards with a thin white rim (meters, progress bars), glossy 3D icons (brain, keycap icon tiles), thin white line art (brain with numbered nodes, outlined caps label boxes with dotted leaders), timeline steppers (dots + Week / Month / 6 Months / 6 Years lighting one at a time), orbit of app icons around a cut-out face.
- Overlays on A-roll: a small icon tracked beside the head; elements cascade around it while the A-roll darkens and defocuses so the overlay becomes the subject.
- Metaphor morphs: a striped sphere slicing apart = 'break it down'; a labelled tank with a tap = motivation.
- Numbered sections: small '1/6' counter top-left; a chapter navigator (icon + number) returns each section.
- Lists accumulate across A-roll interruptions (item 1 stays, item 2 types when you return).

## 5. B-roll
- Drive library first, then Indian stock (Pexels/Pixabay), then Google Flow prompts for stylised scenes the user runs.
- Real Indian street/city footage with a tiny credit bottom-left; drone shots to open places.
- Story/past layers in B&W; graphic scenes in the video's single colour world, desaturated, heavy grain; grade near-B&W behind big type.

## 6. Motion feel
- Calm, editorial, confident: eased moves, constant slow drift, hard cuts on the beat. Graphics build on and leave on the cut.
- Paper/grain/scan-line textures. No bounce-heavy cartoon motion, no glow, no lens flares.
- SFX: soft paper, whoosh on camera moves, pop/click when an item lands, typing ticks for counters.

## Where to render, and cleanup
Render in the cloud, not on the user's Mac; copy to the Mac only when the user says so. After the user finalises the piece, move its references, sources and intermediates on the Mac to the Trash and keep only the final file, skipping anything another task still uses. Full rule in `AGENTS.md` (Working preferences).
